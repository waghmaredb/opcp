# OPCP: AI Adoption Thesis for Financial Services

**Status:** working strategy
**Date:** 2026-10-30

## Thesis

OPCP should not be positioned primarily as an AI governance product. Governance is the entry point. The larger opportunity is to become the **open control layer that lets financial institutions adopt AI aggressively without surrendering ownership of their processes, context or authority**.

> **Any model. Any agent. Any vendor. Your process.**

The core economic promise is AI freedom: a bank can adopt the best capability available today, measure it on its own process data, and replace it tomorrow without rebuilding the process.

## The problem

AI adoption in FSI is constrained by a new form of technology concentration. AI platforms increasingly absorb:

- process logic and workflow;
- institutional context and accumulated know-how;
- prompts, thresholds and exception handling;
- permissions and authority to act;
- evaluation data and decision history.

When these assets become vendor-specific, replacing the supplier becomes a transformation programme rather than a configuration change.

OPCP changes the ownership boundary:

```text
BANK / INSTITUTION
  Process + Context + Authority + Evidence
                 |
          OPCP Control Layer
                 |
      Capability Contracts
                 |
  +----------+----------+----------+
  |          |          |          |
 AI model  AI agent  Vendor tool  Human team
```

## Why this can accelerate AI adoption

The safest way to increase AI adoption is not to add more approval gates. It is to make every AI capability easier to replace.

If replacement is hard:

> bank experiments less -> vendor dependency grows -> risk grows -> adoption slows

If replacement is easy:

> bank experiments more -> capabilities compete -> weak suppliers are replaced -> adoption accelerates

OPCP therefore turns **exitability into an innovation primitive**.

## The strategic product

The product should mature through four layers:

### 1. Exit Readiness

Find the critical process that is most locked into a supplier and quantify the dependency.

### 2. Swap Drill

Prove that a capability can actually be replaced without changing the process, authority model or institutional context.

### 3. AI Capability Control Plane

Run live process traffic through capability contracts, eligibility rules, authority gates, context leases and event logging.

### 4. FSI AI Capability Network

Create a neutral ecosystem where certified suppliers compete to fulfil standardized capabilities and banks can select based on quality, cost, latency, risk and proven swapability.

## The new metric

The north-star metric remains:

> **Number of critical processes with a proven time-to-swap.**

A future product metric should be the **AI Replaceability Index** for each critical process:

- current supplier;
- fallback suppliers;
- percentage of process intelligence held outside the institution;
- test-suite portability;
- authority portability;
- context portability;
- measured time-to-swap;
- concentration exposure.

## Wedge process

The first product demonstration should use **SME credit origination** because it naturally crosses multiple capability types: document extraction, KYC, identity/address verification, trust/risk scoring and human exception handling.

The demo must show the same process running first with Supplier A and then with Supplier B, with the process definition, context, authority and test set unchanged.

## Product principle

> **Trust us because you do not have to trust us.**

A bank must be able to export its process intelligence, run an independent conformant runtime and leave OPCP. Openness is therefore a product feature, not a governance statement.

## Positioning

Avoid:

- "AI governance platform"
- "DORA compliance platform"
- "AI orchestration platform"
- "workflow replacement"

Prefer:

> **The open control layer for AI in financial services.**

Supporting line:

> **Adopt any AI capability. Keep your process memory. Prove you can switch.**

## Strategic moat

The runtime is intentionally open. The defensibility should come from:

1. **Process packs** — tested, regulation-mapped, variation-aware process definitions.
2. **Certified supplier network** — conformant adapters for each capability.
3. **Proven exits** — measured swap drills and historical evidence.
4. **Benchmark data** — anonymized capability performance and replaceability benchmarks, where customers permit it.
5. **Trust mark** — OPCP Conformant as a recognizable procurement signal.

## What must be proven next

1. A real FSI design partner accepts the exit-readiness problem as urgent.
2. A real process can be mapped without replacing the customer's existing BPM/agent stack.
3. Two independent suppliers can fulfil the same capability contract.
4. A swap drill can be executed with measurable time-to-swap.
5. The bank agrees that process intelligence remains portable and institution-controlled.
6. The resulting evidence shortens procurement, resilience or third-party-risk review.

If these six hypotheses are true, OPCP can become infrastructure rather than another point solution.
